# PMI, ITIL, RUP, PRINCE2, and Professional Credentials

## Learning goals

- Distinguish project governance, service management, and software development processes.
- Use frameworks as adaptable bodies of knowledge rather than recipes.
- Evaluate certifications by role relevance, practical application, and current version.

## Different problems, different frameworks

Project management coordinates temporary work toward outcomes, scope, time, cost, risk, and stakeholders. PMI's PMBOK is a body of project-management guidance; it complements, but does not replace, a delivery method. Service management concerns how an organization designs, delivers, supports, and improves services. ITIL provides practices and guidance for that work.

PRINCE2 defines a project-management method emphasizing continued business justification, defined roles, management by stages, and tailoring. RUP (Rational Unified Process) is an iterative, use-case and architecture-centered software development process organized around inception, elaboration, construction, and transition. These models differ in scope and vocabulary; they can be combined selectively, but combining them without clear ownership creates duplicate governance.

Certifications demonstrate study or assessed knowledge against a syllabus; they do not prove architectural judgment or delivery experience. Check the official issuing organization for current exam outline, edition, prerequisites, renewal rules, and language. As of November 2026, PMI publishes PMBOK Guide Eighth Edition; PeopleCert lists current ITIL and PRINCE2 product suites, and the naming/version can change. Use the edition relevant to your organization and verify before exam planning.

## Tailoring to architecture work

Use governance proportionate to risk. A regulated payment migration may need formal stage approvals and audit evidence; an internal prototype may need a short decision record and rapid feedback. Preserve essential controls such as accountable ownership, risk review, change visibility, service readiness, and learning from outcomes.

## Worked example

For a customer-service platform rollout, project management establishes outcomes, budget, risks, and stakeholder decisions. Architecture records system boundaries and migration choices. ITIL practices help prepare support, incident, change, and service-level ownership. An iterative development process delivers usable increments. Avoid separate approval boards that each ask the same question with no decision authority.

## Self-check quiz

1. Does PMBOK prescribe one software-development lifecycle? No; it is a body of project-management guidance and can be tailored.
2. What concern does ITIL primarily address? Management and improvement of IT-enabled services.
3. What are RUP's broad phases? Inception, elaboration, construction, and transition.
4. What does PRINCE2 emphasize? Continued justification, roles, stages, management by exception, and tailoring.
5. Does a certification prove someone can architect a successful system? No; it is evidence of assessed knowledge, not a substitute for experience and outcomes.

## Exercise

Map project management, architecture decisions, software iteration, and service transition for a payment-platform replacement. Identify one useful artifact and accountable owner for each.

### Sample answer

Project lead tracks outcomes, budget, and risks; architect owns ADRs and cross-system views; delivery team iterates through tested increments; service owner prepares support, SLOs, change, incident, and recovery. A steering group resolves business trade-offs while avoiding duplicate technical approvals.
