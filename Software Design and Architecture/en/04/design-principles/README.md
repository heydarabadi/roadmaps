## What you will learn

- Use design principles as decision aids for actual change pressures, not as a checklist.
- Apply SOLID principles to responsibilities, extension points, subtypes, interfaces, and dependency direction.
- Distinguish duplicated business knowledge from harmless textual similarity.
- Decide when composition, encapsulation, or an abstraction reduces change cost and when it adds needless indirection.

## How to study this chapter

Start with the integrated lesson to connect the principles. Then choose a topic page for closer practice. For each proposed design change, write down the pressure it addresses, the simpler option, the expected benefit, and the cost the new abstraction introduces. Use tests and observed change or failure data to review the decision.

The chapter discusses Shopify’s published work on modularizing a large Rails codebase as one context-specific example. Read the company reports and related material in the [reference library](../../references.md#chapter-04); do not treat one company’s constraints or tools as a universal prescription.
